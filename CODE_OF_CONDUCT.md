# Code of Conduct

NativeFrame is a technical project built by people with different backgrounds, languages and levels of experience. We want every issue, review and discussion to be a place where anyone can ask a question, report a measurement or disagree with a design without being made to feel unwelcome.

## Expected behaviour

- Be respectful and assume good faith. Most disagreements are about facts or trade-offs: argue with evidence, not with people.
- Be precise and patient. Explain what you measured and how; ask for the same when something is unclear.
- Give credit. Name the people, projects and pull requests your work builds on.
- Accept that a measurement can prove you wrong, and say so when it does.
- Respect the project's ground rules: no game content, no circumvention, no confidential material (see [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md#ground-rules)).

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, insults, slurs, or personal or political attacks.
- Sexualized language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Discrimination based on age, body, disability, ethnicity, gender identity or expression, experience, nationality, appearance, race, religion, sexual identity or orientation, or any similar characteristic.
- Publishing other people's private information without their explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption: spamming, off-topic flooding, repeatedly reopening settled discussions without new evidence.
- Pressuring anyone to share game files or to break the law.

## Scope

This code applies in every project space: the repository, issues, pull requests, discussions, and any official channel that may be created later. It also applies when someone represents the project elsewhere.

## Reporting and enforcement

Report a problem privately to the maintainer, [@panicDa3m0n](https://github.com/panicDa3m0n), through the contact on the maintainer's GitHub profile. Reports are handled confidentially.

The maintainer may edit or delete content, lock threads, and warn, temporarily block or permanently ban anyone whose behaviour breaks this code, in proportion to what happened and whether it is repeated. Decisions can be appealed by writing to the maintainer.
